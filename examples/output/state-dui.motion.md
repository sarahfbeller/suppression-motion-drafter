## ARGUMENT

### I. The Stop Was Unlawfully Prolonged Beyond Its Original Purpose

A traffic stop becomes unlawful if it is prolonged beyond the time reasonably required to complete the mission of the stop. *Rodriguez v. United States*, 575 U.S. 348 (2015); *Simmons v. State*, 435 P.3d 975 (Alaska App. 2018). Authority for the seizure ends when tasks tied to the traffic infraction are, or reasonably should have been, completed, and an officer may not extend an otherwise-completed stop, even briefly, to conduct a dog sniff or other unrelated investigation absent independent reasonable suspicion. *Rodriguez v. United States*, 575 U.S. 348 (2015). A dog sniff is permissible only if it does not add time to the stop. *Illinois v. Caballes*, 543 U.S. 405 (2005). Where an officer develops reasonable, articulable suspicion of additional criminal activity during the stop's original mission, a brief extension to investigate that suspicion may be justified; the burden is on the government to establish that suspicion arose before, not after, the original mission concluded. *Rodriguez v. United States*, 575 U.S. 348 (2015).

### II. The Defendant Was Denied the Statutory Right to Contact an Attorney or a Relative or Friend Before Deciding Whether to Submit to a Breath Test

A person arrested has the right to telephone or otherwise communicate with an attorney and any relative or friend immediately after arrest. AS 12.25.150(b); Alaska Criminal Rule 5(b). This right requires police to give a person arrested for driving under the influence a reasonable opportunity to contact an attorney by phone before being required to decide whether to submit to a breath test, and violation of this right requires suppression of the test results. *Copelin v. State*, 659 P.2d 1206 (Alaska 1983). This right extends to requests to contact a relative or friend, not just an attorney — the statute draws no distinction between the two on its face. *Zsupnik v. State*, 789 P.2d 357 (Alaska 1990). The remedy for denying a request to contact a relative or friend is exclusion of evidence only where the request served a purpose related to the defense process, such as obtaining advice on whether to take the test. *Zsupnik v. State*, 789 P.2d 357 (Alaska 1990); *Winfrey v. State*, 78 P.3d 725 (Alaska App. 2003).

### III. The Defendant Could Not Knowingly and Intelligently Understand the Advisement of the Right to an Independent Chemical Test

A waiver of the right to an independent test is valid only if it is knowing and intelligent. *Gundersen v. Municipality of Anchorage*, 792 P.2d 673 (Alaska 1990). A defendant with too poor a grasp of English to understand an advisement given only in English may be unable to make a knowing and intelligent decision regarding the right to an independent test, even where the advisement itself followed correct procedure. *Ahtuangaruak v. State*, 820 P.2d 310 (Alaska App. 1991). Where a breath test was administered and is in evidence, the remedy for a violation of this right is suppression of that test result. *Nelson v. State*, 512 P.3d 86 (Alaska App. 2022).
