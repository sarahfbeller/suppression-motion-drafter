### I. The Stop Was Unlawfully Prolonged Beyond Its Original Purpose

A traffic stop becomes unlawful if it is prolonged beyond the time reasonably required to complete the mission of the stop. *Rodriguez v. United States*, 575 U.S. 348 (2015); *Simmons v. State*, 435 P.3d 975 (Alaska App. 2018). Similarly, the investigative methods employed should be the least intrusive means reasonably available to verify or dispel the officer's suspicion in a short amount of time. *Florida v. Royer*, 460 U.S. 491, 500 (1983). Authority for the seizure ends when tasks tied to the traffic infraction are, or reasonably should have been, completed, and an officer may not extend the stop, even briefly, for unrelated investigation unless the officer has independent reasonable suspicion. *Rodriguez v. United States*, 575 U.S. 348 (2015).

### II. The Breath Test Must Be Suppressed Because [Client] Was Denied the Statutory Right to Consult Before Deciding Whether to Submit to a Breath Test

A person has the right to telephone or otherwise communicate with an attorney and any relative or friend immediately after arrest. AS 12.25.150(b); Alaska Criminal Rule 5(b). This right requires police to give a person arrested for driving under the influence a reasonable opportunity to contact an attorney by phone before being required to decide whether to submit to a breath test, and violation of this right requires suppression of the test results. *Copelin v. State*, 659 P.2d 1206 (Alaska 1983).

This right extends to requests to contact a relative or friend when the request served a purpose related to the defense process such as obtaining advice on whether to take the test. *Zsupnik v. State*, 789 P.2d 357 (Alaska 1990).

### III. [Client] Did Not Understand the Advisement of the Right to an Independent Chemical Test

A waiver of the right to an independent test is valid only if it is knowing and intelligent. *Gundersen v. Municipality of Anchorage*, 792 P.2d 673 (Alaska 1990).

When an arrestee fails to understand their right to an independent test due to a language barrier, "the due process clause of Article I, Section 7 of the Alaska Constitution . . . requires suppression of the breath test result." *Ahtuangaruak v. State*, 820 P.2d 310, 311–12 (Alaska App. 1991).

Where a breath test was administered and is in evidence, the remedy for a violation of this right is suppression of that test result. *Nelson v. State*, 512 P.3d 86 (Alaska App. 2022).
